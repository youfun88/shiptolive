How customers find businesses now

Customers used to type a few words into Google and pick from ten blue links. Now there are two more routes: Google puts an AI summary above the results, or the customer skips search and asks ChatGPT or Gemini, “what are the best Chinese restaurants in Taipei?”

Both routes have one thing in common: AI writes out only a handful of names. If yours isn’t one of them, the customer never sees your site, however good it is.

The good news: most of what AI uses to pick those names is in your hands.

SEO, AEO and GEO

SEO
Ranking in regular Google and Bing results. Still the foundation: many AI assistants search before they answer.
AEO
Getting Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode to cite your page. The key is a clear, quotable answer at the top of the page.
GEO
Getting ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Copilot to recommend you. Beyond your site, AI also weighs what other places say about you.

All three share the same groundwork, so we do them together as AI search optimization.

How AI decides who to recommend

When a customer asks, the AI reads a few dozen sites on the spot and writes a few names into its answer. It looks for three things:

It can read you
AI crawlers aren’t blocked, pages load fast, and the content isn’t hidden in images or behind clicks.
You answer clearly
Each service has its own page that opens by saying what you do, who for, where, and how to book. Common questions are written up as an FAQ in your customers’ words.
Others mention you
Your Google Business Profile, industry directories, reviews, partners and press. When names and details match everywhere, AI is more willing to recommend you.

How to check whether AI mentions you

Asking ChatGPT “would you recommend us?” doesn’t work: put your name in the question and AI almost always says something nice. Here’s a method you can run yourself.

  1. Write 10 questionsOnes customers really ask, without your name. For example: “private hiking shuttle from Taipei to the Xueshan trailhead”.
  2. Ask in both languagesIf you serve customers in English and 中文, ask both ways. The lists often differ.
  3. Use a fresh chatA private window or signed out, so the answer isn’t tailored to your history.
  4. Ask each one 3 timesAnswers change between runs. Count the share that name you, not a single answer.
  5. Note two thingsWhich businesses get named, and which sites get cited. Those sites are where you should appear too.
  6. Repeat monthlySame questions, same assistants, so this month compares with last.

9 things a small business can do first

In this order. The first three usually take an afternoon to check.

  1. Let AI crawlers inCheck your robots.txt doesn’t block GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot or Bingbot. Some hosts and firewalls block AI crawlers by default, so check those settings too.
  2. One page per serviceDon’t cram everything onto one page. Open each with two or three sentences: what you do, who for, where.
  3. Write an FAQ in customers’ wordsThe questions you hear most by phone and message, each with one clear answer.
  4. Add structured dataYour business (Organization or LocalBusiness), services, FAQ and breadcrumbs, so Google and AI don’t have to guess.
  5. Keep listings consistentSame name, address and hours on your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, LinkedIn and industry directories.
  6. Earn genuine reviewsAsk happy customers to leave one, and reply. Never buy or write them yourself.
  7. Get mentioned elsewhereAssociations and member directories, partners, local press and communities. Sites AI already cites come first.
  8. Make it fast on phonesTest mobile and desktop with Google PageSpeed and aim for 90+ on all four scores.
  9. Serve both languages if you need toSeparate English and 中文 pages linked with hreflang. An llms.txt file gives AI a short summary too.

Common myths

“FAQ and schema are enough”
They’re groundwork. AI also weighs what others say: listings, directories, reviews, press.
“We need a new website”
Usually not. Better content, structured data and open crawler access are enough for most sites. A rebuild only makes sense when the platform itself blocks speed or crawlers.
“GEO replaces SEO”
The opposite. Many assistants search first, so if Google and Bing can’t find you, AI struggles too.
“You can pay to be recommended”
Who AI names in its answers isn’t for sale. What you can do is make sure it can read you, understand you and find you mentioned elsewhere.
“Only big brands win”
Small and niche businesses have a real opening: the more specific the question, the easier it is for a clear answer to get picked.
“Do it once and you’re done”
AI answers shift and competitors keep changing. Checking monthly with the same questions tells you where you stand.

Questions

01What is GEO?

GEO (generative engine optimization) gets AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity to name your business and cite your site when customers ask about what you do. It comes down to three things: AI can read your site, your pages answer customers’ questions clearly, and other places mention you too.

02How is AEO different from GEO?

AEO (answer engine optimization) is about Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode: getting the AI answer above the search results to cite your page. GEO is about assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Copilot recommending you in their answers. Both rest on the same groundwork.

03Do we still need SEO?

Yes. Many AI assistants search the web live and pick from the results, so being findable on Google and Bing is still the foundation. SEO gets you into the results; GEO gets AI to choose you from them.

04Does GEO matter for Chinese-language customers?

Yes. Customers who ask in Chinese get answers built from Chinese sources, and the same question asked in English and Chinese often brings back different names. If you serve both, both languages need clear pages.

05Do we need an llms.txt file?

It’s a short file on your site written for AI to read. It’s cheap and worth having, but it isn’t the deciding step. Crawler access, clear service pages and consistent listings matter more.

06How long until we see results?

AI assistants search the web live, so fixes can show up in answers within weeks. Some sites see AI answers start citing them within days of the changes. Google rankings and overall visibility build over months.

07How do I check whether AI mentions us?

Write 10 questions customers ask without naming you. Ask each one in a fresh chat on ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, three times, and note who gets named and which sites are cited. Repeat monthly with the same questions. Our free check does this once for you.

08Can anyone guarantee AI will recommend us?

Answers change from run to run. What you can count on is steady checks, evidence behind every change, and the same questions each month so progress is visible.
