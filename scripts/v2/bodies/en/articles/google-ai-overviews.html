What are AI Overviews, and why do they matter?

An AI Overview is the AI-written answer at the top of Google's results, with the websites it used listed beside it. When a customer searches "bookkeeper near me" or "what do I need to register a company", they often see the answer and a few sources in the AI Overview before they ever scroll to the regular results.

For a business, being listed as a source is like being introduced in the most visible spot on the page. If you aren't listed, a finished answer may push you further down. That's why AEO (answer engine optimization) gets more attention every year.

What does Google say is required?

Google's Search Central documentation is direct about it: appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode has no additional requirements and needs no special optimization. If a page is indexed, meets Search's normal technical requirements and is eligible to show a snippet, it can be picked as a source.

Google also notes that meeting every requirement doesn't guarantee a page will be indexed or shown. So be careful with anyone who promises to "guarantee you a spot in AI Overviews".

What 6 things can you actually change?

There's no shortcut, so the work is getting the basics right and making your content answer questions well. This list follows Google's recommended practices, in the order we use when we optimize client sites:

  1. Let Google crawl your site. Check robots.txt and your hosting or CDN settings so Googlebot isn't blocked by accident.
  2. Make important pages easy to find with internal links. Service pages, pricing and your FAQ should be reachable from the home page or the menu.
  3. Put important content in text. Don't show prices, your process or service areas only in images.
  4. Give each question one direct answer. Use the customer's wording as the heading and answer in the first sentence, for example "How much does bookkeeping cost?"
  5. Make structured data match the page. If you add FAQ or business markup, it must match the text people can see on the page.
  6. Keep your Google Business Profile up to date. Hours, address and services should be current. This is one of the practices Google lists too.

What are the 5 most common mistakes?

When we review client websites, the problem is usually not doing too little. It's working in the wrong direction. These 5 mistakes come up most:

  • Thinking you need a special tool or file. Google has said plainly that AI Overviews need no special AI files or markup. Before spending money on those, check that your pages are indexed and your content is solid.
  • Writing only the home page. AI Overviews answer specific questions, such as "how much does bookkeeping cost". A home page is usually too general. Specific questions need specific pages.
  • Blocking crawlers by accident. After a redesign, a hosting move or new CDN protection, Googlebot may be blocked. The site looks fine to you, but Google can't read it.
  • Mass-producing similar articles. Generating dozens of near-identical articles with a tool won't add visibility, and it can make it hard for Google to tell which page matters. Google keeps stressing helpful content made for people first.
  • Judging from one result. AI Overviews change over time and from search to search. Not appearing today doesn't mean you won't next month. Track the same searches on a schedule.

Avoid these, then work through the 6 changes above one at a time. That works better than chasing new tricks. If you also want ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI assistants to recommend you, much of the work overlaps: see how to get ChatGPT to recommend your business.

FAQ

Do I have to pay or apply to appear in AI Overviews?

No. There is no way to apply or pay for AI Overviews. Google picks sources from pages that are indexed and eligible to show a snippet. What you can do is make sure your pages are indexed and answer questions clearly.

Do I need llms.txt or special structured data to appear in AI Overviews?

According to Google's documentation, you don't need new machine-readable files, AI text files or special structured data. Structured data still has other uses, but if you add it, it must match the text people can see on the page.

How do I know if my site appears in AI Overviews?

The most direct way is to search the words your customers use and see whether there's an AI Overview and which sites it cites. The Performance report in Google Search Console also includes these appearances. To track many searches over time, use a tool that checks them on a schedule.

Can I keep my site out of AI Overviews?

Yes. Google says nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet or noindex control what Search shows, and these settings also apply to AI Overviews. But most businesses want to be cited, so don't add them casually.
