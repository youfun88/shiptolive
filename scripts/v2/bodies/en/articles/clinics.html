How do patients ask AI about clinics?

Patients ask AI fuller, more specific questions than the keywords they type into Google. They state the need, the place and their conditions all at once. Some example searches:

SpecialtyWhat a patient might ask AI
Dental"Recommend a dental implant clinic near me." "Is there a children's dentist in my neighborhood?" "Which clinic has the most experience with clear aligners?"
Aesthetic"Recommend a clinic for picosecond laser in my city." "What should I know before my first Botox treatment? Where should I go?"
Rehabilitation"A rehab clinic near me that's open in the evening." "Where should I go for frozen shoulder rehab?"

AI usually names only a few clinics in its answer. When we present to clients, we often say: in an AI answer, if you aren't mentioned, you don't appear at all. There's no second page of results to scroll to.

How does AI pick a clinic to recommend?

When AI picks clinics, it looks for information that answers the patient's question directly, and it checks several sources against each other. It comes down to three things:

  • Treatments and doctors' specialties are clear. If a patient asks about implants, AI needs to find an implant page on your website, plus the doctor who does them and their background.
  • Location, hours and directions are complete. For conditions like "in my neighborhood" or "open in the evening", AI compares them directly with the address and hours you list.
  • Third-party information matches. Your name, address and phone number on your Google Business Profile, review sites and medical directories should match your website.

AI reads several websites before it writes an answer. Clinics with clear information and consistent sources are more likely to make the list. To understand how this works behind the scenes, read how AI decides which business to recommend.

What should a clinic's website include?

The most important thing is that every main treatment has a page that answers patients' questions directly. Each page should cover:

  • Who this treatment suits: which conditions it can help with, and when it isn't a good fit.
  • Process and time: how the first visit assessment works, roughly how many sessions it takes, and how long each one lasts.
  • Doctors and equipment: the specialty and experience of the doctor in charge, stated as facts. Skip adjectives like "the leading expert".
  • How fees work: if you don't publish prices, explain how the cost is estimated and that you quote after an assessment.
  • Common questions: turn the questions patients ask most in the exam room into Q&A. This is the format AI cites most easily. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.
  • Visit details: address, nearest transit stop, parking, opening hours and how to book.

How should a clinic manage its Google Business Profile and reviews?

For a local clinic, the Google Business Profile is one of the outside sources AI uses most. Check that:

  • Your name, address and phone number match your website exactly, and your hours (including closures and public holidays) stay up to date.
  • You chose the right category for your specialty, and listed your main treatments as services.
  • You reply to reviews regularly. Protect patient privacy when you reply: don't discuss anyone's condition in a public reply.

What do medical advertising rules mean for AI search?

Clinic website content counts as medical advertising in many places. In Taiwan, it is regulated by the Medical Care Act. Elsewhere, check your local rules. When you work on AI search optimization, keep to a few principles:

  • State facts objectively, and don't promise results. Avoid words like "guaranteed", "100%" or "permanent cure".
  • Don't exaggerate or compare. Don't claim to be "the best in the country", and don't put down other clinics.
  • Be careful with reviews and cases. Before you use patient testimonials or before-and-after photos, confirm that this is allowed and that the patient has agreed.

This is a general reminder, not legal advice. If you're unsure about any content, ask the regulator or a legal advisor first.

FAQ

Does AI search optimization for a clinic break medical advertising rules?

The work is about writing your treatments, your doctors' qualifications, your location and your hours clearly and correctly. Patients need this information anyway. Keep the content objective, don't promise results, and avoid exaggerated or comparative wording. In Taiwan, also follow the Medical Care Act's rules on medical advertising; elsewhere, check your local rules. If you're unsure about a phrase, ask the regulator or a legal advisor first.

Does AI look at Google reviews when it recommends a clinic?

Yes, it takes them into account. When AI searches, it often reads your Google Business Profile, maps and review sites. How many reviews you have, what they say, how recent they are, and whether your business details match your website all affect whether AI includes you.

My clinic offers many treatments. How should the website handle that?

Give each main treatment its own page. Say who it suits, how the treatment works, how many sessions it takes, aftercare and common questions. Patients ask AI very specific questions, and a dedicated page is easier to cite than one page that lists everything.
