What is AEO?

AEO stands for answer engine optimization. The goal is to make your website a source that AI cites when it gives an answer. An answer engine is a search feature that writes out the answer instead of just listing links. The most common ones are AI Overviews and AI Mode at the top of Google's search results.

Say a customer searches "how much does a bookkeeper charge in [city]" (an example search). Google may first show a short, organized answer with a few cited sources next to it. The websites listed as sources get seen before the customer even scrolls down. That spot is what AEO competes for.

How do AEO, SEO and GEO compare?

The difference is where you show up. The base is the same for all three: a website that can be read, content that answers clearly, and consistent information on other sites.

NameThe spot it competes forTypical situation
SEO (search engine optimization)Rankings in regular search resultsA customer types keywords into Google and clicks a site near the top
AEO (answer engine optimization)Cited sources in AI Overviews and AI ModeGoogle shows an answer first, with source websites below or beside it

How do answer engines change the way customers find a business?

Customers no longer need to open several websites and compare them. The answer engine organizes the answer for them first. Before, a site in 4th or 5th place still had a chance to be seen as the customer scrolled. Now the answer sits at the top, and the few websites it cites become the first options the customer sees.

When we present to clients, we explain it as a value chain: better content, more visibility, more website visits, more bookings and orders. When your content is clear, Google and AI find and cite you more easily, and the visits follow. The chain only keeps turning if you keep working on it.

What does AEO work actually involve?

The core of AEO: answer the questions customers ask in clear text on your website, and make sure Google can read it. In practice:

  • Check that pages are indexed. Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in Search before it can appear in AI Overviews. Use Google Search Console to check that your important pages are indexed.
  • One question, one direct answer. On service pages and FAQs, use the question as the heading and answer it in the first sentence. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.
  • Put important content in text. Don't show pricing details, service areas or your process only as images. Google recommends providing important content as text.
  • Keep structured data consistent with the page. Google says you don't need special structured data to appear in AI Overviews. But if you add it, it must match the text people can see on the page.
  • Keep business information up to date. Google also recommends keeping information such as your Google Business Profile current.

How do you write content that answer engines cite?

Content that gets cited usually has one thing in common: the reader gets the answer from the first sentence alone. An answer engine has to answer in very little space, so it prefers paragraphs it can quote directly that make sense on their own. These are the contrasts we use most when we rewrite content for clients:

Less likely to be citedMore likely to be cited
"We offer comprehensive, customized professional services.""We handle bookkeeping, tax filing and company registration for small businesses in [city], with a monthly accounts report."
A heading that says "Our services"A heading that asks the customer's question: "What documents do I need to register a company?"
Pricing that only says "Contact us", with no explanationAn explanation of how fees are worked out and what affects them
Key information only in images or PDFsKey information written as page text, with images only as support

Also, keep each topic on one page instead of spreading it across several similar articles. With clear content and consistent information, Google can more easily tell which page answers the question best.

One more point people often miss: your content must match reality, and it must be kept up to date. If your hours, service area or way of pricing change, change your website too. When an answer engine cites outdated information, it causes trouble for your customers and for you. We suggest checking your important pages at least once a quarter and showing an "updated" date on the page.

Where should a small business start with AEO?

Measure first, then improve. Start by listing the 5 questions customers ask most. Search each one on Google and see whether an AI Overview appears and who it cites. Then add content for the questions where you aren't cited, and re-check with the same questions every month.

FAQ

Are AEO and SEO the same thing?

No, but they share the same base. SEO aims to rank high in search results. AEO aims to get your content cited as the source of an answer in features like AI Overviews. A website that can be read, with clear and accurate content, helps both, so in practice they are usually done together.

Do I need special code or files for AEO?

According to Google, you don't need special machine-readable files, AI text files or specific structured data to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. The page only needs to be indexed and meet the usual technical requirements for Search. What matters is still clear, useful content.

Should I start with AEO or GEO?

If most of your customers use Google Search, start with AEO. If they often ask ChatGPT or Gemini directly, work on GEO at the same time. The groundwork for both overlaps a lot, so start with an AI visibility check to see where the bigger gap is.

Can AEO guarantee my website will be cited in AI Overviews?

No. Google also says that even if you meet every requirement, there is no guarantee your page will be indexed or shown. What you can do is make your content easier to read and understand, and keep tracking which searches show an AI Overview and whether it cites you.
