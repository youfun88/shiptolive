Key points
  • When AI answers a customer's question, it looks for sentences it can use as the answer. A well-written FAQ is exactly that.
  • Write the questions the way customers ask them, not in your company's internal terms. Put the answer in the first sentence, then add details.
  • Answer one thing per question, and make each answer make sense on its own, because AI often takes just one part of a page.
  • The FAQ must be visible on the page. Structured data only helps, and it must match what the page says.

Why does AI cite FAQs so often?

Because an FAQ is already "one question, one answer", which is almost exactly how customers ask AI. Say a customer asks ChatGPT "how long does it take to build a website?" The AI looks for content that answers that question directly. If your FAQ has the question "How long does it take to build a website?" and the first sentence gives the answer, the AI can easily use it in its reply, and may even name your website as the source.

When we present to clients, we often explain it like this: SEO gets you ranked high in Google search, AEO gets Google's AI Overviews to cite your website, and GEO gets ChatGPT and Gemini to recommend you. All three rest on the same base: writing what customers want to know, clearly and correctly. An FAQ is the most direct way to do that. To learn these terms first, read what AEO is and what GEO is.

Where do the FAQ questions come from?

The best questions are the ones customers have already asked you. You don't need to guess. Collect them from these places:

SourceHow to find themExamples
Phone, chat apps, emailGo through customer questions from the last three months and pick the ones that repeat"Are you open on weekends?" "Can I get an invoice for my company?"
Sales and front-desk staffAsk them which five questions they answer most every day"What should I bring to my first consultation?"
Google Search ConsoleIn the Performance report, look for search terms phrased as questions"how much does X cost" "best X"
Ask AI directlyAsk ChatGPT "what do customers usually ask when looking for X?" as a supplementComparison, price and process questions

Use your customers' words. A customer asks "do dental implants hurt?", not "information on patient comfort during our implant treatment". AI matches content against the customer's own words, so the closer your wording, the easier the match.

How should you write FAQ answers? 5 rules

An answer should stay complete and correct even when AI takes just that one part. Follow these five rules:

  • The first sentence is the answer. Give the conclusion first, for example "It takes 4 to 6 weeks" or "Yes, we issue invoices", then add reasons and exceptions.
  • Answer one thing per question. If your answer starts to say "also" or "in addition", it usually should be two questions.
  • Name who and where. Write "Example Design Studio offers ... in your city", not "We offer ...". When AI takes a snippet, "we" loses its meaning.
  • Be specific, but only about what you know for sure. Opening hours, service areas, process steps and what to prepare can all be stated clearly. If you aren't sure of a number, leave it out rather than get it wrong.
  • Keep it to 2 to 4 sentences. Too short and there's no information. Too long and the AI has to shorten it itself, and may miss the key point.

Should you add FAQ structured data?

You can, but don't expect it to work on its own. What matters is still the content people can see on the page. In 2026 Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in search (the questions and answers that expand below a search result). And Google's official position on AI Overviews is that no special structured data is needed.

Even so, FAQPage structured data is still a clear way to tell machines which sentence is the question and which is the answer. If you add it, follow one rule: the questions and answers in the structured data must match the text visible on the page. It looks roughly like this (example values):

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "How is the cost of a website calculated?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "We quote per project. Once your needs are confirmed, we send a written quote."
    }
  }]
}
</script>

FAQ

How many questions should an FAQ page have?

There's no fixed number. What matters is that every question is one customers really ask. On a typical service page, 3 to 6 questions is enough. If you have many, group them by topic, so unrelated questions don't end up on the same page.

Should FAQs be on one page or on each service page?

Do both. Put questions about a specific service at the bottom of that service's page. Put general questions that apply to all services, such as payment methods and service areas, on one main FAQ page. That way, when AI reads a service page, it finds the related answers right there.

Once my FAQ is written, how long before AI starts citing it?

Usually weeks to months, and there's no guarantee. Search engines and AI need to read the page again first, and information about your brand on other sites also affects the result. Check with the same set of questions on a regular schedule and compare the results over time.

Can I use AI to help write my FAQ?

You can use AI to collect questions and write a first draft, but you must check the facts in every answer yourself, such as your service scope, process and pricing. If wrong information gets cited by AI, customers end up with the wrong idea.
