Before you start: what questions should you track?

Write down the 5 questions your customers ask AI most. These are your optimization targets. Use the same set every time you check, so you can see progress.

Write them the way customers talk, not in marketing language. An interior design firm in San Diego, for example, might track "recommended interior designers in San Diego", "who should I hire to redo a small apartment" and "how much does an interior designer charge". Adding a place and a specific need makes the question closer to what people really ask.

How do you do each week?

Week 1: check where you stand

Ask each of the 4 AI assistants your 5 questions and log the answers in one table: did it name you, which competitors did it name, which websites did it cite. The cited websites matter. They show where AI goes for information, and they're places you may want to appear later. The full method is in how to check whether ChatGPT and Gemini recommend your business.

Also check your website's robots.txt. If it blocks AI crawlers, AI can't read your site.

Week 2: add answers to your website

For each question, make sure your site has a passage that answers it directly. Give the answer in the first sentence, then add detail. Each service page should at least say:

  • what you offer and which areas you serve,
  • who it's for, and who it isn't for,
  • how you charge (you can explain how you quote; you don't have to list prices),
  • the process and how long it takes,
  • how to contact you or book.

Turn the questions customers ask most into an FAQ section, with each answer starting with the answer itself.

Week 3: make outside listings match

Before AI recommends a business, it looks at business profiles, directories and reviews. This week, fill in your Google Business Profile and Bing Places, and list yourself in 1 or 2 industry directories. The key is that your business name, address, phone and website are the same on every platform as on your site. When the details match, AI can trust them more easily.

Week 4: re-check and track

In Google Search Console, request indexing for the pages you changed. Then check again with the same questions and compare with week 1. The first re-check often shows little change. That's normal, because search engines and AI need time to read your site again. What matters is that from this week on, you re-check once a month.

What are the 5 most common mistakes?

These are the mistakes we see most when working with clients. Avoid them and your 30 days are less likely to go to waste:

  • Writing questions in marketing language. Customers don't ask for "end-to-end brand solutions". They ask for "a recommended branding agency in San Diego". Use your customers' words.
  • Changing the questions every time. Once the questions change, the numbers can't be compared. Keep your targets for at least a few months.
  • Judging from one result. AI answers change by nature. Look at the trend across several checks.
  • A beautiful website with no specifics. Without service areas, how you charge and who you serve, AI has a hard time connecting you to a customer's question.
  • Details that don't match across platforms. One address on the website, another on your business profile, an old phone number in a directory: AI finds it harder to tell that these all belong to the same business.

One more thing people overlook: don't mass-produce unreviewed content for AI. A wrong price or service in an article hurts more than your AI visibility. It hurts customer trust too. Someone who knows the business should read every piece before it goes live.

How do you plan it if one person does it all?

At many small businesses, the owner or one staff member looks after the website on the side. If time is short, give each week one focus:

  • Start with the page that matters most. Usually that's the service customers ask about most. You don't need to redo the whole site at once.
  • Write down the date of every change. When you re-check, you'll know which change may have helped.
  • If you get stuck, stop at the check. Even if you only finish week 1, you know how AI describes you today and who it recommends. Every later decision starts there.

FAQ

How can a small business show up more in AI search?

List the 5 questions your customers ask AI most and check where you stand. Then add content to your website that answers those questions directly. Next, make your Google Business Profile and directory listings match your website. Finally, re-check with the same questions on a regular schedule.

Will AI recommend me after 30 days?

Not necessarily. 30 days is enough to lay the groundwork, but search engines and AI need time to read your site again, and outside information takes time to build up. Expect to see changes over several months.

Can I do this myself without a technical background?

Mostly, yes. Checking AI answers, writing service descriptions and FAQs, and filling in your Google Business Profile need no coding. The more technical parts, such as structured data and AI crawler settings, can go to whoever runs your website.

Do I have to check all four AI platforms?

At least check the platforms your customers use most. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity often give different answers, so record each one separately to see where you need work.
