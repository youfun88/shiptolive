Why should you be careful when choosing a GEO agency?

GEO (generative engine optimization) is still new. Many services are traditional SEO with a new name, and methods and results vary a lot. Traditional SEO has well-known measures, such as rankings and clicks. GEO has to measure something else: whether AI answers name you. If an agency has no way to measure that, you can't tell whether the money is well spent.

We often use one example with clients. Ask ChatGPT and Claude the same question, "Which Chinese restaurant in Taipei do you recommend most?", and you get different lists, each with only a few names. That tells you two things. Answers differ by platform, so each one has to be measured on its own. And there are only a few spots, so being on the list or not makes a big difference.

What are the warning signs?

If you hear any of these, ask more questions before you decide:

  • "We guarantee AI will recommend you" or "We guarantee the #1 spot." AI answers change with the question, the time and the platform. No one can guarantee a result. What an agency can promise is regular measurement and improvements backed by evidence.
  • A single score, with no raw results. If you can't see what AI actually said and who it named, you can't tell whether a gain is real or the scoring just changed.
  • Publishing lots of content without your OK. Large amounts of auto-generated, unreviewed content can get your prices or services wrong and can hurt trust in your brand.

What will you need to do once you hire an agency?

With a good GEO agency, your part should be small, but it isn't zero. Usually it's three things:

  • Answer the facts only you know. What you offer, prices, opening hours, the areas you serve. AI pays close attention to these details when it recommends a business, and an agency shouldn't guess them.
  • Review drafts. New content and changes go to a review page, and you approve them or ask for changes. Anything that changes what you tell customers must go through you.
  • Tell them about changes any time. A new phone number, new hours, new prices: you should be able to tell the agency whenever, and they update your website and listings.

Before you sign, ask: "How much of my time will this take each month? Where and how do I review things?" If the answer is "You don't need to do anything", ask who checks content before it goes live. Results depend a lot on your input: the more complete your answers and the faster your reviews, the faster things improve.

Also ask who handles technical changes. Page titles, meta descriptions and structured data don't change what you say, so the agency usually handles them. But each one should still come with a reason, so you know why it changed.

How should you shortlist agencies?

Start small to learn where you stand, then decide on a longer contract:

  1. Ask for a check first. Have the agency ask AI your customers' questions once and see what they give you: a record of real answers, or general advice.
  2. Compare two or three agencies. The point isn't who sounds best. It's whose measurement, review process and reporting are clearest.
  3. Confirm your part. GEO needs facts only you know, such as what you offer, prices and where you work. The more complete your input, the faster things improve.

FAQ

How can I tell if a GEO agency knows what it's doing?

Ask them to explain how they measure: which customer questions they use, which AI platforms they check, how often, and whether you can see the raw result of every check. An agency that can't explain how it measures will find it hard to prove results.

A GEO agency says it can guarantee AI will recommend me. Should I believe it?

Be careful. AI answers change with the question, the time and the platform, and no one can guarantee that a given platform will recommend you. More reliable promises are regular checks, a reason for every change, and numbers you can compare over time.

Can an SEO company do GEO?

Yes, but check that they actually test AI answers, and that they work on Google AI Overviews and on what other sites say about you. Ranking for keywords alone is not GEO.
