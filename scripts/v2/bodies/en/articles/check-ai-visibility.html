Why check before you start optimizing for AI search?

Without a measurement you don't know what to fix, and you can't show whether a fix worked. Many businesses find on their first check that AI never names them, even though they thought they were well known, or that AI names them but gets their services or location wrong.

An example we use with clients: ask AI "Which Chinese restaurant in Taipei do you recommend most?" ChatGPT and Claude give different lists, and each lists only a few places. The customer's shortlist is whatever AI lists. So the first step is to see how AI answers your customers' questions today.

How do I choose the 5 questions?

Each question should meet two conditions: it is something your customers ask, and the answer lists businesses. Pick one or two of each type:

TypeExample (an accounting firm in San Diego)
RecommendationWhat are the best accounting firms in San Diego?
Comparing and hiringWhich San Diego accountant is good for startup bookkeeping and taxes?
Price and bookingHow much does a San Diego accountant charge to set up a company? Who do you recommend?

Avoid asking "Is [your company] any good?" Customers who don't know you yet never ask that. What you want to test is whether AI recommends you to people who haven't heard of you.

How do I check, step by step?

  1. Log out, or open a new chat. That keeps the AI from using your earlier conversations, so the result is closer to what a customer sees.
  2. Make sure the AI is searching the web. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity can all search the web; when they do, the answer usually links its sources.
  3. Ask one question at a time, word for word. Ask every AI the same 5 questions, without changing the wording.
  4. Record the answer. Whether you are named, in which position, which competitors are recommended and which websites are cited. Save a screenshot.
  5. Mark what is wrong. If AI names you but gets your address, services or prices wrong, note it separately. That is usually the quickest thing to fix.
  6. Repeat every month. Use the same questions the same way, so you can compare before and after.

How do I keep the log?

A spreadsheet is enough. One row per question, one column per AI assistant, like this (an example):

QuestionChatGPTGeminiClaudePerplexity

Keep a second tab for "competitors recommended" and "websites cited". Both lists are useful: the cited websites are the sources AI relies on, so you can check whether your business is listed on them.

What are the most common self-check mistakes?

The most common problem is checking a different way each time, so the results can't be compared. Avoid these four from the start:

MistakeWhy it's a problemWhat to do instead
Changing the questions each timeDifferent questions, results you can't compareKeep the same 5 questions for six months; log any new ones separately
Asking only one AIDifferent assistants often give different listsAsk at least ChatGPT and Gemini, ideally all four
Asking "Is [your company] any good?"Only people who already know you ask thatAsk questions that make AI recommend businesses, like "best [service] in [city]"
Recording only "named or not"You lose the clues for what to fixAlso record the competitors recommended and the websites cited

How do I read the results?

The point is not whether you were named this time, but why, and what to do next:

  • Never named: first make sure AI can read your website and that your services and area are stated clearly, then add your Google Business Profile and listings on outside directories.
  • Named by some assistants but not others: compare the websites each one cites and get onto the sources you are missing from.
  • Named, but with wrong details: correct your website and your profiles on other platforms so they all match. Later checks usually improve, but it takes time.
  • One question is much harder than the rest: your website is missing a page that answers it. Write an article or an FAQ answer for it.

What is the difference between checking it myself and using a tool?

The method is the same: ask AI the same questions and record the results. The difference is scale and consistency. A tool can ask at a fixed time, with fixed questions, on several platforms at once, and keep every result, which makes trends easier to see. Checking yourself is a good way to see where you stand first.

AI gives a different answer every time I ask. What should I do?

AI answers do change. Ask the same question a few times to see the overall pattern, and repeat the check every month the same way. Judge by the trend over months, not by a single answer.

AI names my business but doesn't link to my website. Does that matter?

Being named means the AI knows you. But if it cites other websites, its sources are not your own site. Check whether your website has a page that answers that question directly; that makes it more likely to be cited.

My results are poor. Where do I start?

First fix anything AI gets wrong about you, then add the answers your website is missing, then work on outside sources such as your Google Business Profile, industry directories and reviews. In that order you usually see improvement fastest.
