Key points
  • GEO (generative engine optimization) is the work of getting AI assistants to name and recommend your business when they answer customers' questions.

  • GEO and SEO share the same base. The goal differs: SEO aims for rankings, GEO aims to be written into the AI's answer.
  • The first step isn't changing your website. It's checking: does AI name you today, and who does it recommend?

Why should a small business care about GEO now?

More customers no longer scroll through search results. They ask AI directly: "Which accounting firms do you recommend near me?" or "What's a good restaurant for a team dinner?" The AI reads many websites and writes one answer. That answer usually names only a few businesses.

That means one thing: businesses the AI names go straight onto the customer's shortlist. Businesses it leaves out may never be seen. In Google's results, a business in fifth place still had a chance. In an AI answer, if you aren't named, you aren't there.

The name "GEO" comes from a 2023 research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization". Its authors tested which ways of writing content are more likely to be cited by generative AI. Since then the term has been widely used for the work of getting AI to recommend you.

How does AI decide who to recommend?

AI assistants that can search the web, such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, read online content as they answer and then summarize it. How they pick businesses comes down to three things:

  • Your website can be read. AI crawlers must be able to read your site. If your site blocks AI crawlers, or the content sits inside images or behind a login, the AI can't read it.
  • It answers questions clearly. When a customer asks "who's good at X in my city?", the AI looks for content that answers directly: what you do, which areas you serve, who you're a fit for, how you charge and how to contact you. The more specific you are, the more likely you are to be cited.
  • Other sites mention you too. AI looks at third-party sources: your Google Business Profile, industry directories, news coverage, forum threads and customer reviews. If only your own website says you're good, that carries little weight.

What is the difference between GEO, AEO and SEO?

The three have different goals but the same base, so in practice they are usually done together:

NameGoalWhere it shows up
SEO (search engine optimization)Your website ranks high in search resultsRegular Google and Bing results
AEO (answer engine optimization)Your content is cited as the source of an answerGoogle AI Overviews, AI Mode, featured snippets
GEO (generative engine optimization)Your brand is named and recommended by AIAnswers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity

Where should a small business start?

Go in this order: measure first, then improve.

  1. List 5 questions your customers would ask AI. Use your customers' words, not marketing language. For example "recommended dentist near downtown", not "comprehensive oral care".
  2. Check how AI answers today. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity those questions. Write down whether they name you, which competitors they recommend and which websites they cite.
  3. Add the answers your website is missing. For each question where you aren't named, add service details, FAQs, the areas you serve and who you're a fit for. Make sure AI crawlers can read the pages.
  4. Work on your information elsewhere. Complete your Google Business Profile, industry directory listings and reviews, so other sites mention you and match what your website says.
  5. Re-check with the same questions on a schedule. Compare once a month. That's the only way to see whether your changes work.

What does GEO look like for a real business? An example

Here's an example to show the whole process. Say "Design Firm A" takes on interior design for small apartments. Its website has lots of project photos but almost no text.

  1. Check first. It asks ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude 5 questions such as "recommended interior designer for small apartments in my city". The AI doesn't name Firm A. It mostly cites design platforms and forum posts.
  2. Add the missing answers. It adds a "small apartment design" service page: what sizes it suits, which neighborhoods it serves, how the process works and how quotes work. It turns customers' common questions into an FAQ.
  3. Get mentioned elsewhere. It updates its Google Business Profile and its listings on design platforms, so the services and areas match the website.
  4. Re-check the same questions every month. It watches whether AI starts naming Firm A and which page it cites, then decides what to add next.

What are the most common GEO myths?

  • GEO is not buying ads. You can't pay to be recommended in an AI answer. What you can do is make your information clearer and easier to cite. See how to get ChatGPT to recommend your business.
  • GEO is not done once. AI answers change and competitors keep adjusting, so you need regular checks and updates. For how to track progress, see how long GEO takes.
  • GEO is not just fixing your home page. What gets cited is usually service pages, FAQs and outside listings that answer specific questions, not the slogan on your home page.

FAQ

What is the difference between GEO and SEO?

SEO aims to rank your website high in Google's search results. GEO aims to get ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI assistants to name and recommend you in their answers. Both rest on the same base: a website that can be read, with clear and accurate content. That is why they are usually done together.

Can GEO guarantee that AI will recommend me?

No. AI answers change with the question, the time and the platform, and no one can guarantee that a given platform will recommend you. What you can do is measure whether AI names you, find out why it doesn't, improve your website and your information on other sites, and track what changes.

Does a small business need GEO?

If your customers use AI to find products, services or suppliers, it's worth starting. AI answers usually name only a few businesses, and being on that short list or not makes a big difference. Start with an AI visibility check to see where you stand, then decide.

How long does GEO take to show results?

Usually months. After you change your website, search engines and AI assistants need time to read it again, and mentions on other sites take time to build up. Check with the same set of questions on a regular schedule and compare the results over time.
