How do parents ask AI about classes?

When parents look for a class for their child, they give the child's age, what they need and a convenient area all at once. Example searches look like this:

TypeWhat a parent might ask AI
School subjects"Recommended English tutoring centers in [district]" or "My kid is falling behind in middle school math. Any small-group tutoring in [city]?"
Music, art and activity classes"Coding classes for elementary school kids in [city]" or "Recommended kids' piano lessons in [neighborhood]"
Adult classes"Evening Japanese classes near [main station]"

AI usually lists only a few schools. Parents who see that list will often book a trial class straight from it.

How does AI pick a tutoring center or class?

AI looks for information that matches the parent's conditions: subject, age, location and time. It comes down to three things:

  • Clear courses and students. "Small-group pre-algebra prep for 7th graders, 8 students per class" matches a parent's question far better than "tutoring in all subjects".
  • Full location and time details. Each branch's address, the nearest transit stop and class times. AI compares these directly against conditions like "in [district]" or "on weekends".
  • Trial classes and reviews. Whether you offer a trial class, how to sign up, and what other parents say.

What course information should your website include?

Give each main course or age group its own page. Each page should cover:

  • Who it's for: grade, level and the problem the student wants to solve.
  • Class format: class size, hours, materials and whether there's an online option.
  • Teachers: each teacher's background and teaching strengths, stated as facts.
  • Times and locations: each branch's address, how to get there and the class schedule.
  • Trial classes and sign-up: the steps, what to bring and how to get in touch.
  • Common parent questions: Q&A like "What if my child can't keep up?" or "Can we join mid-term?" is the content AI cites most easily. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.

What should your business profile, reviews and local listings show?

  • Google Business Profile: every branch needs one, with a name, address, phone number and class hours that match your website.
  • Parent reviews: when a course ends, invite parents to leave a specific review, and keep replying to reviews.
  • Local groups and family sites: mentions in parent communities, local media or family activity listings are outside sources AI also uses.

Where should a school start?

Go in this order: find out where you stand, then improve step by step. When we explain this to clients, we describe it as a chain that keeps itself turning: clear content makes it easier for AI and Google to find and recommend you, more parents get in touch, and trial classes and sign-ups grow with them.

  1. List the 5 questions parents ask most. Use parents' words, such as "recommended elementary English tutoring in [district]", not "complete English education".
  2. Check how AI answers today. Ask the main AI platforms those questions. Write down whether they name you and which schools they recommend.
  3. Add the answers your website is missing. For each question where you aren't named, add course pages, teacher details and common parent questions.
  4. Set up a business profile for each branch. Keep the details consistent with your website, and invite parents to leave specific reviews.
  5. Re-check with the same questions every month. Compare the results. That's how you learn which changes work.

What should you watch for in enrollment copy?

  • Don't promise grades, rankings or admission results. Explain how you help students through your course design and teaching methods.
  • Get consent before you publish student results or lists of top students, and protect the privacy of minors.
  • In Taiwan, the rules for setting up and advertising a cram school are set by the authorities, so follow their official notices. Elsewhere, check the rules that apply where you teach.

FAQ

Does a tutoring center's website have to list its fees?

Not necessarily, but it helps to explain how fees are worked out, for example by class format, number of lessons or term. When parents ask AI roughly how much it costs, schools with a clear explanation of fees are more likely to be cited. If you say nothing, AI can only cite other sources.

Do parent reviews affect AI recommendations?

Yes. When AI searches, it often looks at Google Business Profiles and reviews. How many reviews you have, how specific they are (for example, naming a course or a teacher), how recent they are, and whether your business details match your website all affect whether you get recommended.

Is the approach the same for music and art classes as for tutoring centers?

The basics are the same: state the courses, who they are for, the location, class times and how trial classes work. Music and art classes can show more photos or videos of student work, but each photo still needs a text description, because AI mainly reads text.
