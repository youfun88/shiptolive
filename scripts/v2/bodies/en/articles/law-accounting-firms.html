How do clients ask AI about lawyers and accountants?

People looking for a lawyer or an accountant usually come with a specific problem, and they care a lot about whether the firm has handled something similar. They ask AI questions like these:

TypeWhat a client might ask AI
Lawyer"Recommend a lawyer who handles inheritance in my city." "What kind of lawyer do I need for an employment dispute?" "Who can I talk to about a divorce agreement?"
Accountant"Recommend an estate tax accountant in my city." "Which accounting firm do you recommend for setting up a startup?" "Who can help me report overseas income?"

Each of these questions includes a specialty and a place. AI looks for the firm that handles exactly this kind of case, not the biggest firm. For small and mid-sized firms that focus on one area, that's an opportunity.

How does AI judge a firm's expertise?

When AI decides whether a firm is worth recommending, it mainly looks for specific, verifiable information about its expertise:

  • Clear practice areas. "We focus on estate and gift tax" matches a client's question far better than "we offer full-service finance and tax solutions".
  • The people and their background. State the lawyers' or accountants' names, years in practice, education and experience, and languages, as facts.
  • Outside mentions. Professional association directories, articles in legal or tax media, talks and publications let AI see you in other places too.

What should a firm's website include?

  • One page per practice area. Use the client's question as the heading, for example "How do I file estate tax, and what documents do I need?", and give the answer in the first paragraph.
  • Service process: how the first consultation works, what to bring, and a rough timeline.
  • How you charge: you don't have to publish prices. Explain how you estimate fees and how consultations are charged.
  • Profiles of your people: give each lawyer or accountant their own profile page that states their specialties and background.
  • Common questions and articles: answer the questions clients ask most, and show the author and the date it was last updated. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.

If you serve clients in more than one language, publish a version in each language and set the correct language tags. Structured data (for example the LegalService and AccountingService types) also helps AI understand your services. See how structured data helps AEO and GEO.

How do directories, media and reviews help a firm?

Professional services depend heavily on outside trust signals. Check that:

  • Your firm's name, address and website are the same in bar association, CPA association and other professional directories as on your own website.
  • Your Google Business Profile is complete, with the right category, and you reply to reviews regularly (without revealing case details).
  • When you're invited to write, speak or give an interview, you ask the host to link to your firm's website.

What do professional conduct rules mean for AI search?

Lawyers and accountants each have their own professional conduct rules, and these limit how you can promote your practice. The general principles are:

  • Describe your specialties and background as facts. Don't promise case outcomes or tax savings.
  • Don't use unverifiable or comparative wording such as "the strongest" or "the highest win rate".
  • When you mention cases, protect client privacy and don't reveal anything that could identify them.

This is a general reminder, not legal advice. The rules that actually apply are those of your professional association and regulator.

What should a practice area page look like? An inheritance example

Clients usually ask AI about something very specific, such as "a lawyer who handles inheritance in my city". If a practice area gets only one sentence on your site, AI can hardly tell whether you really specialize in it. Use this structure for each practice area page:

  1. Describe the service in one sentence. For example: "We help heirs divide an estate, file estate tax and resolve related disputes."
  2. Who it's for. List common situations, for example heirs living abroad, family members who disagree, or real estate in the estate.
  3. How the process works. Explain the steps from the first consultation to completion.
  4. Who handles it and their background. Say who is responsible and what relevant experience they have, in a way that can be verified.
  5. How fees work. Explain how you charge, for example per case or by the hour. You don't have to give amounts.
  6. Common questions and how to book. Answer the 3 to 5 questions clients ask most, and show how to book a consultation.

FAQ

Can a law or accounting firm do AI search optimization?

Yes. AI search optimization is about writing your practice areas, the background of the people who handle the work, your service process and your contact details clearly. Clients need this information anyway. Keep the content factual, don't promise outcomes, don't put down other firms, and follow your profession's conduct rules.

Does a firm's website have to list fees?

No. Many firms quote case by case, and you can say that clearly: explain how fees are calculated, how the first consultation is charged, and what documents you need before you can give an estimate. When AI answers "roughly how much does it cost?", a clear explanation of how you charge is more likely to be cited than saying nothing.

Does publishing articles help with AI recommendations?

Yes, as long as the content is specific, accurate and answers questions clients really ask. For example, a clear explanation of a common estate tax, company setup or employment dispute question, with the author and date shown, is more likely to be cited by AI than a general promotional post.
