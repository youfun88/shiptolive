How do customers ask AI about restaurants and shops?

When people look for a restaurant or a shop, they tell AI the occasion, the area and how many people are coming, all in one question. Some example searches (the words in brackets stand for a real place):

TypeWhat a customer might ask AI
Restaurants"a restaurant in [neighborhood] for a team dinner", "a romantic restaurant in [neighborhood] with private rooms"
Leisure"indoor activities for groups in [city]", "indoor places for kids in [city] on a rainy day"
Retail and services"a recommended coffee bean shop in [neighborhood]", "where can I order a custom cake in [city]"

Why do different AI assistants recommend different places?

We often use this example when we present to clients: ask AI "which is the most recommended Chinese restaurant in [city]?" ChatGPT lists one set of restaurants. Ask Claude the same question, and it lists a different set. The two lists don't match, and each names only a few places.

This tells you two things. First, checking one AI isn't enough: check ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity separately. Second, the list isn't fixed. If you manage your information well, you have a chance to get on it.

What should you check on your Google Business Profile?

For local businesses, the Google Business Profile is one of the sources AI uses most. Check each item:

  • Matching basic details: your business name, address, phone number and website link are exactly the same as on your website, booking platforms and social media.
  • Up-to-date hours: update days off, public holidays and temporary changes. AI answers "are they open now?" directly.
  • Category and attributes: choose the right main category, and tick attributes such as good for groups, private rooms or pets allowed.
  • Photos and menu: upload photos of the space, the food and the menu, and describe your signature items in text.
  • Reviews: invite customers to share specific experiences, and reply to every review, including negative ones.

What should a restaurant or shop add to its website?

  • Occasions you suit: group meals, dates, families, company events. Say so directly, and give how many people you can seat and whether you have private rooms.
  • What makes you special: be specific, for example how your signature dish is made and what goes into it, not just "delicious".
  • Menu and price range: publish a text menu, not just a photo of it. AI can't read the words inside an image.
  • Booking and getting there: how to book, the nearest train or metro station, and parking.
  • FAQ: questions like "can I bring my dog?" or "do you have vegetarian options?" are the easiest for AI to cite. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.

What happens after AI recommends you?

Being recommended by AI is valuable because it's a chain: each step drives the next. When we present to clients, we break it into four links:

LinkWhat the business does
1 Better contentWrite your menu, specialties, occasions and opening hours clearly and correctly.
2 More visibilityGoogle and AI find, cite and recommend you more easily.
3 More website visitsMore customers open your website or business profile to check the menu and how to get there.
4 More bookings and visitorsBookings, phone calls and walk-ins go up.

The chain only keeps turning if you keep at it: update your business details right away, keep replying to reviews, and add new menus and events to both your website and your business profile.

What should you avoid?

  • Don't buy reviews or offer discounts for good reviews. It breaks platform rules, the reviews may be removed, and it may carry legal risk.
  • Don't let your details contradict each other. If your address or hours differ from platform to platform, AI trusts your information less.
  • Don't rely on social media posts alone. Social content often needs a login to see, so AI may not be able to read it. Your website and business profile are still the base.

FAQ

My shop doesn't have a website. Can AI still recommend it?

It can, because AI often uses Google Business Profiles, maps and reviews. But a shop with a website can describe its menu, what makes it special, what occasions it suits and how to book. That gives AI more content to cite, and usually a better chance of being recommended.

Do more reviews make AI more likely to recommend me?

The number helps, but it isn't the only factor. AI also weighs whether reviews are specific (which dish, what occasion), whether new reviews keep coming in, whether you reply to them, and whether your business details match your website.

Why do ChatGPT and Gemini recommend different places?

Each AI reads different sources and sums them up in its own way, so the same question can get different lists. That's why you should check each of the main AI platforms, not just one.

How do I know whether AI recommends my shop right now?
