How do overseas buyers ask AI?

When buyers in other countries look for suppliers, they often ask AI in English and add conditions such as the product, the process and the certifications they need. These example searches are for suppliers in Taiwan:

SituationWhat a buyer might ask AI
Finding a manufacturer"CNC machining supplier in Taiwan with ISO 9001", "aluminum die casting manufacturer Taiwan"
Finding a trading company"Taiwan trading company for industrial fasteners", "who exports bicycle parts from Taiwan"
Comparing suppliers"Taiwan vs Vietnam suppliers for precision parts, which companies to contact?"

What sets B2B buying apart: buyers do a lot of homework before they contact a supplier. The list AI gives them may well be the companies they send their requests for quotes to.

How does AI choose which suppliers to recommend?

When AI recommends suppliers, it looks for specific information that matches the buyer's conditions directly:

  • Products and process capabilities: what you make, in which materials, with which processes, and to what precision.
  • Certifications and export experience: quality certifications such as ISO, and the main markets and industries you have exported to.
  • Complete English information: whether your English website, contact person and quote process are clear.

Write your product specs, certifications and capacity as clear text, so Google AI Overviews and other AI assistants have content they can cite.

What should your English website include?

For a B2B company, the English website is the main source AI uses to learn about you. We suggest it includes:

  • One page per product line: product type, materials, processes, spec ranges and the industries it's used in.
  • Manufacturing capabilities: equipment, how you describe your capacity, and your quality control process.
  • Certifications: list the names of the certifications you hold, with information buyers can verify.
  • Order terms: explain minimum order quantity, samples and lead times. Even if the answer is "quoted on request", say what information you need to quote.
  • RFQ process: what drawings or specs to send with a request for quote, how soon you reply, and who to contact.
  • FAQ: answer the questions buyers often ask, such as whether you accept small orders or offer OEM / ODM.

Structured data (Organization, Product) can help AI understand your company and products (see how structured data helps AEO and GEO). Your website can also offer an llms.txt file that gives AI a summary of your company and products.

Which outside sources should match your website?

  • Industry directories and B2B platforms: keep your company name, address and product categories the same as on your website.
  • Trade show listings: your company description in exhibitor directories, with a link to your English website.
  • Associations and media: industry association member listings and trade media coverage are outside sources AI uses.
  • LinkedIn company page: an English company description, products and contact details that match your website.

What are the most common problems?

  • Only a PDF catalog: if key information is only in PDFs or images, AI may not be able to read it. Write it as page text too.
  • Only the home page is in English: the product pages have no English version, so buyers who ask in English can't find you.
  • Names that don't match: your company name in your own language and in English is written differently across directories, so AI may not be able to tell it's the same company.

How should you structure an English product page?

When overseas buyers ask AI, the AI needs to read specs, certifications and contact details directly on your English pages. Here is a product page structure you can use as is:

  1. Title and a one-line summary. The product name plus its core spec, such as material, size range or use. The first paragraph answers "what is this, and what is it used for?"
  2. Spec table. List material, dimensions, tolerances, surface finish and so on in a table, not only in an image or PDF.
  3. Certifications and standards. List the certifications you actually hold. Where useful, add the certificate number or how to verify it.
  4. Production and shipping. As far as you can share it, describe capacity, lead times, minimum order quantity and export experience. Don't publish numbers you aren't sure of.
  5. FAQ. The questions buyers ask most about samples, packaging, payment and shipping. See how to write an FAQ that AI will cite.
  6. How to request a quote. A clear RFQ form or email address, and the languages your contact person speaks.

FAQ

Does a B2B company need AI search optimization?

It's worth doing if your buyers use AI to build a list when they look for suppliers. B2B buyers often do their homework before they contact a supplier, and AI answers usually name only a few companies. Being on that list gives you a chance to get onto their list of companies to ask for a quote.

Is a website in our own language enough?

If you want buyers from other countries, have a complete English version, not just a translated home page. Overseas buyers ask AI in English, and AI mainly cites English content. Your product pages, specs, certifications and contact details all need to be in English.

We have lots of product specs. Do they all need to go on the website?

At least write out, as page text, the key specs, materials and processing capabilities of your main product lines, the order sizes you accept and how you handle lead times. You can offer the full spec sheet as a download, but keep the key information in the page text, because AI doesn't always read files.
