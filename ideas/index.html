Where are people showing up, and where is it already crowded?

Growth alone isn't enough. We want a category that's growing and where AI isn't everywhere yet.

What does the search data say about the ideas?

Pulled from Google Trends on October 11, 2026: US web searches, October 2021 to now. It confirmed the home idea, narrowed it to mold and water damage, and showed GLP-1 is bigger than we assumed.

Google Trends shows relative interest, not search counts, and 2026 covers January to October only. Some rising searches for broad terms like "home repair" are local ads and quiz questions, so we only quote the rising searches that are clearly real people.

Which three ideas are worth testing first?

Picked for how quickly they could reach people through search, not just how big the market is.

01
Fastest growing

Snap a photo, find out what's wrong at home

Take a photo of a leaking water heater, a mold spot, or a part you can't name. The app tells you what it is, how serious it is, roughly what it costs to fix, and whether to do it yourself or call a pro.

Start with mold and water damage, the two problems with the fastest-growing searches, and answer the health worry first. Then grow into free web pages, one per problem, and a paid home-maintenance tracker with reminders.

  • House & Home
  • Photo search
  • SEO pages
  • Web first, then app

Why it fits

  • Fastest-growing category in the data
  • Google searches for "water damage" are up 222% and "mold in house" up 65% since 2021
  • Rides the shift to searching by photo (25B+ Lens searches a month)
  • Few AI apps here yet
  • Every problem is its own search, so each page can bring in Google traffic
  • The maintenance tracker gives people a reason to come back
02
Testing now

A GLP-1 companion for muscle and life after the drug

About 1 in 9 US adults now takes a GLP-1 like Zepbound or Wegovy. There are already plenty of apps for logging shots. The two problems they mostly leave alone: 15–45% of the weight lost can be lean mass, including muscle, and in one major trial people regained about two-thirds of it within a year of stopping.

So the app focuses on those: a daily protein target, short strength workouts, and a 12-week plan for coming off the drug. The first test is a free protein calculator with a waitlist for that plan. 100+ sign-ups in 2–3 weeks means we build it.

  • Health & Fitness
  • Subscription
  • Daily habit
  • Live test

Why it fits

  • Searches for "zepbound" rose from 4 to 87 since 2023, more than double "home repair"
  • Record $4.5B in-app spending; people already pay
  • Daily tracking builds the habit AI apps lack
  • Users gather in big, easy-to-find communities (r/Zepbound alone has about 211k weekly visitors)
03
Fast but small

Hundreds of niche calculator and converter pages

Generate a page for each specific question in one niche. It's the quickest way to get Google traffic, but each visitor is worth little, so it works best as a way to bring people to idea 01 or 02.

  • Utility
  • SEO
  • Ships in days

Why it fits

  • People search these when they need an answer right now
  • Cheap to build on our existing web stack

Skipped on purpose: general chatbots (big players win), short-drama video apps (need a content studio and ad budget), and finance (regulation and trust slow everything down).

What do people ask about picking an app to build?

01What app should I build in 2026?

Our short answer: build a free web tool that answers something people already search for, then grow it into an app. Our research points to three ideas, best first: a photo app that tells you what's wrong at home, starting with mold and water damage; a GLP-1 companion for protein, strength and coming off the drug; and pages of niche calculators and converters.

02Which app categories are growing fastest?

In Peekly's August 2026 data, ranked by median 7-day growth in new reviews on the App Store and Google Play: House & Home (+63.6%), Maps & Navigation (+54.7%), Art & Design (+51.2%), Photo & Video (+50.8%) and Health & Fitness (+50.2%). Reviews stand in for downloads, so treat this as a signal, not a count.

03Should a new app be an AI app?

AI alone won't keep people. AI apps earn 41% more per payer in their first year ($30.16 vs $21.37), but fewer keep paying: after 12 months, 6.1% of monthly subscribers to AI apps are still paying vs 9.5% for other apps, and 21.1% vs 30.7% on annual plans (RevenueCat). And 61.4% of Photo & Video subscription apps already use AI. Pick a category where AI is rare, with a reason to come back, like reminders, tracking or a recurring problem.

04Why start a home app with mold and water damage?

Because those searches are growing. Google searches for "water damage" are up 222% and "mold in house" up 65% from 2021 to 2026, while "dishwasher not draining" fell 30% (Google Trends, US). The fastest-rising mold searches are about health, like "symptoms of mold toxicity", so the first question to answer is "Is this mold, and could it be making me sick?"

05Which idea are you testing first?

The GLP-1 companion. The first test is the free GLP-1 protein calculator, with a waitlist for the plan behind it. If 100 or more people sign up in 2 to 3 weeks, we build the app. Try the protein calculator

06How can I check demand for an app idea before building it?

In one afternoon. Look up the idea's search terms in Google Trends with the "Rising" filter on. Type the keywords into App Store search, note what autocomplete suggests, and read the 1 to 3 star reviews of the top 3 apps. Count how often the same question comes up each week on Reddit. Then put up a single working page and see whether people find it and use it before you build the app.
