Your customers are asking AI who to hire.

More people now skip the search results and ask an AI assistant: “Who's the best accountant in San Diego?” The assistant replies with two or three names. If yours isn't one of them, that customer never reaches your website, however good it is.

Most businesses don't know what these assistants say about them. Each engine searches its own way, the answers change from day to day, and English and Chinese results differ. So we built a tool that measures it.

Asking ChatGPT yourself won't show you what customers see.

It's the first thing most owners try. But one chat can't measure what your customers are told, for four reasons.

Different question
You ask “Is Acme Accounting any good?” Your customers ask “Who's the best accountant in San Diego?” and never say your name. Once a business is named in the question, AI almost always finds something nice to say. The real test is whether it names you when nobody else does.
AI can't report on itself
Ask “Would you recommend my business?” and the AI guesses what it might tell someone else. It isn't checking anything, and its guesses tend to be polite.
One answer is one sample
Ask the same question twice and you can get different names. One reply tells you very little, so we run each question several times and report how often you're named.
Tailored to you
AI adjusts its replies to your location, your past chats and what it remembers about you. And you're checking one engine, while your customers use four.

How it works.

  1. Ask like a customer15 real customer questions in English and 中文, sent to ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude with live web search, from your customers' cities.
  2. Read every answerWas your business named? In what position? Who was named instead, and which websites did the AI cite?
  3. Check your websiteStructured data, llms.txt, whether AI crawlers are blocked, page speed: what AI engines rely on to understand and trust a site.
  4. Repeat monthlySame questions, same engines, same cities, so each month's numbers compare with the last.

Built and run in-house.

Python and LangGraph, calling each engine's own web-search API so the answers match what real users see. Every answer is saved, every run logs its cost, and the scoring is checked against businesses whose results we already know.

It has run in production since October 2026 as part of our tech marketing service: the monthly check feeds the to-do list, and the results live in each client's dashboard. A self-serve version, where you enter your business and get the report by email, is the next step.
